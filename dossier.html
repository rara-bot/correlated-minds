Correlated Minds

Three regulators have named correlated AI behaviour a top financial-stability risk. Two 2026 papers model its consequences. Every one of them treats the amount of correlation as an assumed number — because nobody has measured it.

The finding that reframes the search

The obvious topics closed during 2026

I surveyed the AI×finance frontier against what ~180 hours can actually produce. Adversarial attacks on LLM traders, lookahead-bias benchmarks, demographic bias in LLM credit and investment advice, and algorithmic collusion each now carry multiple 2026 papers — one of them an NBER working paper under revision at AER. That closure is itself the useful result. It says the field has moved past “can AI do finance?” and “can AI be fooled?” to a harder question it has not yet equipped itself to answer.

The gap

A policy debate running without its central number

The FSB (Oct 2025), the Bank of England (Jul 2026) and the IMF (Jul 2026) have each named correlated behaviour across institutions running similar AI models as a top-tier stability risk. The BoE says it is “pursuing work on simulation methods… to understand conditions under which AI agents could demonstrate correlated behaviour or herding.”

Meanwhile the measurement tools were built in 2025–26 — error correlation, effective ensemble size — and applied to general forecasting, LLM-judge panels and prediction markets. The two literatures have not met. Supplying the missing number is a measurement problem, and measurement is precisely what a disciplined three-month program with no institutional data access can do better than anyone.

The thesis in one identity

N_eff = M / (1 + (M − 1) ρ̄)

Recommended program — candidate A

How many independent minds is the market actually getting?

Finance already knows one version of this pathology: correlations go to 1 in a crisis, so diversification fails exactly when it is needed. The hypothesis is that AI cognition has the same pathology, for a mechanistic reason.

When evidence is strong, models are driven by the evidence. When evidence is weak, ambiguous or novel, they fall back on priors — and because they share enormous overlap in pretraining data, those priors are shared. Errors should therefore decouple in calm states and lock together in stressed ones. If that holds, the effective number of independent minds in an AI-mediated market is not a constant. It is a function of market state that declines into the tail.

One methodological commitment underwrites everything: we correlate errors, not outputs. Output similarity is inflated by the task merely having a right answer. Error correlation nets that out and isolates shared wrongness — the only kind that is dangerous. The closest theory paper draws exactly this distinction, then does not measure it.

Hypotheses — pre-registered before collection

H1 · Conditional collapse

N_eff falls as stress and ambiguity rise

Every existing measurement is unconditional — a single scalar. The conditional structure is the entire fragility story, and it is untouched.

FALSIFIED IF stress-quintile coefficient is null at 95% after multiple-testing correction.

H2 · Shared-prior mechanism

The collapse is prior fallback, not noise

In high-ambiguity states the consensus should be predictable from base rates and largely insensitive to the evidence actually presented.

FALSIFIED IF consensus tracks evidence strength equally across ambiguity terciles.

H3 · The diversification illusion

Prompt diversity buys nothing; family diversity buys a little

If true, every risk manager running an “ensemble” on one vendor’s model holds a diversification strategy that does nothing. Cheapest test, shortest path to real-world consequence.

FALSIFIED IF intra-model ΔN_eff is statistically indistinguishable from cross-family ΔN_eff.

Elevation — from measurement to discovery

What turns this into a Grand Award

Honest diagnosis: the spine above is “we measured a parameter well.” That earns respect in a journal and rarely wins a fair. Awards go to projects with a different shape — a discovery, a mechanism, a prediction that was confirmed, an instrument you can hold. Four elevations close every gap, and three of them cost nothing.

E1 · Highest value · $0

A pre-registered prediction, tested out-of-sample

Every paper in this literature fits a correlation to data already in hand. We have what none of them do — a panel that hasn’t happened yet. Calibrate on Weeks 1–5, then publicly commit a hashed numerical prediction: “on the next macro surprise above the 80th percentile, ρ̄ will exceed X and N_eff will fall below Y.” Weeks 6–15 test it on events that did not exist when it was made.

A CONFIRMED CONDITIONAL FORECAST IS A CATEGORICALLY STRONGER CLAIM THAN A FITTED COEFFICIENT. IF IT MISSES, WE REPORT THE MISS — THAT IS WHY WE PRE-REGISTER.

E2 · Mechanism · $0

Measure correlation at three levels, not one

Decision (do they reach the same call — what everyone measures), distribution (do they hold the same uncertainty — free, from token logprobs), and representation (do they encode the situation the same way — CKA over hidden states, local forward passes on small open-weight models).

The closest theory paper builds its entire argument on the distinction between representation homogeneity and forecast overlap, claims the former drives instability — and never measures either. We can test its central premise directly.

FORWARD PASSES ONLY, NOT GENERATION — RUNS ON 8 GB AT 1.5–3B SCALE. STATED AS A MECHANISM PROBE ON SMALL OPEN MODELS, NOT ASSERTED AT FRONTIER SCALE.

E3 · Reframed after checking · $0

Lineage as a risk predictor — not a discovery

Model phylogeny is already claimed (PhyloLM; LLM DNA; When Agents Look the Same, Apr 2026, which finds within-family pairs more similar than cross-family across 18 models). So we don’t claim the tree — we use it: does known training lineage predict which pairs decorrelate under stress and which collapse together?

STILL OPEN, AND DIRECTLY ACTIONABLE — IT GIVES A RISK MANAGER A RULE FOR WHICH MODELS ACTUALLY BUY INDEPENDENCE.

E4 · The poster weapon · ~$15

The independence meter

Build it as a deployable instrument. A judge walks up, types a financial scenario, and seven models answer live. The dial reads 6.2. Then they drag the ambiguity slider — evidence gets weaker — and watch the models converge and the dial collapse toward 1.4 in front of them.

THIRTY SECONDS, VISCERAL, UNFORGETTABLE. IT ALSO REFRAMES THE PROJECT AS INSTRUMENTATION — A CLASSIC GRAND-AWARD SHAPE.

What we cut to make room

Elevations add ~90 hours; two trims fund them, and both are improvements. The retrospective arm shrinks to a validation set — it was always the weaker, contaminated evidence. And the agent simulation becomes a closed-form result: state price-impact and volatility scaling analytically as a function of N_eff instead of simulating an order book. Strictly better, because judges are rightly sceptical of simulation black boxes and a closed-form result cannot be accused of tuned parameters.

Net ~290 hours, unchanged. Budget unchanged at $200.

The hook, before and after

Before: “I measured how correlated AI financial decisions are.”

After: “Seven AI models from seven different companies — and under market stress they stop being seven. I predicted when it would happen before it happened, traced it to how the models represent the situation internally, and built the instrument that measures it.”

Discovery, confirmed prediction, mechanism, instrument. If ρ̄ reaches ~0.85 under stress, N_eff ≈ 1.15 — seven minds, one answer. We don’t know yet whether it will. That is the point of measuring.

Why it survives scrutiny

What we concede

The metric is not ours — correlated LLM errors and effective ensemble size are established. Live contamination-free evaluation is not ours either. Both get cited prominently, and we claim neither.

What is genuinely ours

Financial decision tasks with economic loss functions — correlation on a Brier score is not correlation on a portfolio decision, because the second is weighted by money at risk. State-dependence, which no existing measurement attempts. Empirical calibration of the parameter the theory literature and three regulators currently have to guess. And transmission to prices, so the result carries an economic magnitude rather than just a coefficient.

The contamination defence

The standard kill-shot against LLM-finance work is that the model already knows the answer; a 2026 ensemble paper calls contamination “a pervasive confound.” We defeat it by construction: the prospective panel forecasts events that have not happened yet at the moment of the call. No amount of pretraining leaks an outcome that does not exist.

Workstreams

WS0

Instrument

Weeks 0–1

Multi-provider async client, pinned model IDs, full logging, cost accounting with a hard cap, content-addressed cache. Pre-registration frozen and timestamped before any data is collected. Local Python is 3.9.6 with no numpy or pandas — the venv is a real task, not an assumption.

WS1

Prospective panel — the spine

Weeks 1–10, daily

~30 decisions/day × 7 model families (21 pairs) on unresolved events: scheduled macro releases, upcoming earnings surprises, Kalshi/Polymarket contracts with a market-implied benchmark, 8-K materiality ground-truthed on realised reaction, and a fixed-scenario allocation giving decision correlation weighted by money at risk.

WS2

Retrospective panel

Weeks 2–5

Historical tasks for statistical power and for stress states the live window may not contain. Contaminated by construction, so used only for power and cross-checking — never as a standalone claim. The WS1↔WS2 gap measures contamination’s effect on apparent diversity, which is a bonus result.

WS3

Econometrics

Weeks 5–9

N_eff with block-bootstrap CIs; state-dependence on VIX, realised vol, expectation dispersion, |surprise|, news volume, novelty; multiple-testing correction; shared-signal vs shared-error decomposition.

WS4

Transmission model

Weeks 7–10

Measured N_eff(s) enters a noisy rational-expectations market model. The point is the feedback loop: stress → higher ρ̄ → lower N_eff → more correlated order flow → more price impact → more stress. If measured state-dependence is strong enough, the loop self-reinforces above some AI participation share — a critical threshold. That phase diagram is the headline chart.

WS5

Mitigation

Weeks 8–10

Tests H3 and ranks what actually buys independence: cross-family > architecture > retrieval grounding > persona > prompt > temperature. Yields a practitioner recommendation with numbers attached.

WS6

Artifacts and writing

Weeks 9–11

Open dataset, open-source neff toolkit, preprint, 20-page competition paper, poster, talk.

Budget

The whole program fits in $200. Two findings came from checking rather than assuming, and both shape how.

Claude Pro cannot be the instrument. It is a consumer subscription, not API credits — and the distinction is methodological, not just billing. The study needs seven different model families with pinned IDs and logged raw responses; H3 is specifically the intra-model versus cross-family contrast, so a single-vendor panel cannot test it at all. Pro stays valuable for building the harness and running the analysis; the measurement needs separate credits.

Local inference is largely closed on this machine — M1 with 8 GB of RAM. That reverses my earlier “run open-weight locally to save money” suggestion: 8 GB runs a 3B–7B quantised model too slowly for a 105-day panel, and that tier isn’t representative of deployed systems anyway. Open-weight families go to hosted inference instead, which is cheap enough that it barely moves the total.

The $200 build — and why the constraint improves the instrument

The cap is $200 on top of Pro. That works, and the redesign is better science rather than a degraded version. Three moves.

1 · Kill the reasoning tokens. The original estimate assumed 1,500 output tokens per call including extended thinking, and output bills at roughly 5× input — it dominated everything. Force a structured JSON response instead: probability, direction, confidence, one-sentence rationale. About 200 tokens. This is what we should have specified anyway — we correlate decisions, not essays, and free-text rationales vary in length and style across families, injecting noise into the exact quantity we're estimating. Then run a 10% subsample with extended reasoning on, which is where H2's mechanism analysis lives. We need rationale text from ~1,800 calls, not 18,000.

2 · A mid-tier roster, which is also more representative. Seven families at mid-tier with one frontier anchor. The justification isn't only cost: mid-tier models are what institutions actually deploy for high-volume tasks — cost matters to them too. Sonnet 5 anchors the top end so we can still test whether tier affects correlation.

3 · Caching and batching, as before — a 4,000-token stable prefix at ~0.1× on reads, and 50% off via the Batch API, since WS1 only needs the call made before the event resolves.

One infrastructure decision worth flagging

The panel must fire every day for 105 days, and a laptop that sleeps is not a dependable scheduler. Running it as a GitHub Actions cron job is free — and the commit history becomes a timestamped, tamper-evident record that every forecast was registered before its event resolved.

That isn’t a workaround, it’s a methodological asset. The strongest objection to any LLM-forecasting result is “how do we know you didn’t run this after the fact?” A public commit log answers it in one line.
