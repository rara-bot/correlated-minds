# Submission Targets & Why the Paper Exists

**[F]** = verified directly · **[S]** = from search snippet, re-verify before relying on it

---

## 1. "Why do we need a paper if ISEF is a poster and a talk?"

Fair question — and at the *international* level you're partly right.

**What ISEF actually requires [F]:** a **250-word abstract**, forms, and the poster. A project data
book and research paper are **not required at the international level** — but Society for Science
says they are **"strongly recommended for judging purposes."** Regional and local fairs *may* require
them, and many do.

So at ISEF itself, the paper is optional. Four reasons to write it anyway:

**1. Other venues require it outright.**

| Venue | Paper requirement |
|---|---|
| **JSHS** | **Required** — min 5–6 pages, max 20 including appendices, 250-word abstract, 12pt double-spaced **[F]** |
| **Regeneron STS** | **Required** — max 20-page research report **[F]** |
| **Davidson Fellows** | Portfolio built around a significant completed work |
| **Any conference** | The paper *is* the submission |

**2. Everything else is derived from it.** The abstract, poster, ten-minute talk and conference
submission are all compressions of one source document. Write the paper once in December and
January–May is adaptation, not authorship. Skip it and you write the argument from scratch four
times, badly.

**3. It is how you come to own the argument.** ISEF judging is an interview. Judges probe: *why that
control? what would falsify this? why should I believe the correlation isn't an artifact?* Writing
the paper is the process that forces every one of those answers to exist. Students who skip it get
caught on the third question. This is the real reason, and it's not close.

**4. It is the artifact that outlives the season.** A poster is gone in a day. A paper on arXiv is
citable, permanent, timestamped, and is the thing you attach to an email asking to present.

**Verdict:** the December paper is the master document. Write it once, properly.

## 2. Timeline fit

Your regional fair — **submission early January, presenting late January** — fits a 31 December
finish almost exactly. One week between the finished paper and the submission.

**One flag worth acting on now:** **Regeneron STS 2027 closed 5 Nov 2026 [F]** — before this project
finishes. STS 2027 is out.

- **If a senior:** STS is unavailable this cycle. ISEF + JSHS + Davidson are the ladder.
- **If a junior:** **STS 2028 (deadline ~Nov 2027) is the single best target available.** You'd
  submit with a 60-week panel instead of 15, plus a confirmed out-of-sample prediction and, likely,
  a published preprint. That is a categorically stronger application than anything finishable by

## 6. Poor fits — don't waste effort here

Most high-profile economics and finance competitions are **exam- or simulation-based, not research**,
so a research project cannot be entered at all:

| Venue | Why not |
|---|---|
| Wharton Global High School Investment Competition | A 10-week **trading simulation** [S]. Not a research venue |
| National Economics Challenge | Exam and quiz-bowl format |
| Harvard Pre-Collegiate Economics Challenge | Exam and quiz-bowl format |
| International Economics Olympiad | Exam-based |
| USACO / IOI | Competitive programming, unrelated |
| **MIT THINK Scholars** | **Funds projects not yet built** (proposals, 1 Jan deadline) [S]. Our project will be *finished* — wrong category |

That last one is a common trap: THINK looks like a perfect prestige fit until you notice it's a
proposal competition.

## 7. The clinch-factor strategy

Trophies are one axis. The thing that actually separates a strong high-school project from one that
reads like **real research** is external validation from the adult research world:

1. **arXiv preprint in October** — before any competition. Every subsequent application can say *"a
   preprint of this work is publicly available and timestamped."*
2. **An accepted workshop paper at ICLR or NeurIPS** — this is the single highest-leverage item on
   the list. Blind-reviewed, genuinely competitive, and vanishingly rare at high-school level.
3. **Sigma Xi** — explicitly open to high schoolers, real honor-society prestige, low barrier.
4. **Then the competition ladder** — ISEF, JSHS, Davidson — where the preprint and workshop
   acceptance are now *evidence* rather than claims.

The order matters. External validation first makes every competition application stronger, and it
costs nothing but the submission.
